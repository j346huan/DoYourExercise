No. Take $A=\mathbb{Q}[x^2-1]\subseteq B=\mathbb{Q}[x]$, $\mathfrak{n}=(x-1)$, and $\mathfrak{m}=(x^2-1)$ in $A$. The element $x$ satisfies the monic equation $T^2-(x^2-1)-1=0$, so $B$ is integral over $A$.

The element $1/(x+1)$ lies in $B_{\mathfrak{n}}$. If it were integral over $A_{\mathfrak{m}}$, multiply its monic equation by a power of $x+1$ and clear the denominators in $A_{\mathfrak{m}}$. This gives a polynomial identity $d(x^2-1)+(x+1)h(x)=0$, where $d(0)\neq0$. Evaluating at $x=-1$ gives $d(0)=0$, a contradiction.
