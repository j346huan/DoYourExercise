Suppose $\mathfrak{a}=\bigcap_i\mathfrak{q}_i$ is a minimal primary decomposition, with $\mathfrak{p}_i=r(\mathfrak{q}_i)$. Given a prime $\mathfrak{p}$, choose $f\notin\mathfrak{p}$ belonging to every $\mathfrak{p}_i$ not contained in $\mathfrak{p}$, by taking a product of elements of $\mathfrak{p}_i\setminus\mathfrak{p}$. Then $f$ avoids the remaining $\mathfrak{p}_i$. Localization and \exref{4.15} give $S_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{a})=S_f(\mathfrak{a})=(\mathfrak{a}:f^n)$ for large $n$, proving (L1). For (L2), each $S_n(\mathfrak{q}_i)$ is either $A$ or $\mathfrak{q}_i$, and can change from the former to the latter at most once. There are finitely many components.

Conversely, set $\mathfrak{a}_0=\mathfrak{a}$. If $\mathfrak{a}_{i-1}\ne A$, choose $\mathfrak{p}_i$ minimal over $\mathfrak{a}_{i-1}$ and put $\mathfrak{q}_i=S_{\mathfrak{p}_i}(\mathfrak{a}_{i-1})=(\mathfrak{a}_{i-1}:x_i)$ with $x_i\notin\mathfrak{p}_i$. As in \exref{4.17}, $\mathfrak{q}_i$ is primary and $\mathfrak{a}_{i-1}=\mathfrak{q}_i\cap(\mathfrak{a}_{i-1}+(x_i))$. Choose $\mathfrak{a}_i$ maximal among ideals containing $\mathfrak{a}_{i-1}+(x_i)$ for which
\[\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{q}_1\cap\cdots\cap\mathfrak{q}_i\cap\mathfrak{a}_i.\]
Such a maximal ideal exists by Zorn's lemma, since unions of chains preserve this equality. We have $\mathfrak{a}_{i-1}\subsetneq\mathfrak{a}_i$.

Put $S_n=A\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^n\mathfrak{p}_i$. Since $x_i\in\mathfrak{a}_n\setminus\mathfrak{p}_i$ for each $i\le n$, prime avoidance gives $S_n\cap\mathfrak{a}_n\ne\varnothing$. Each $\mathfrak{q}_i$ for $i\le n$ is already $S_n$-saturated, so
\[S_n(\mathfrak{a})=\mathfrak{q}_1\cap\cdots\cap\mathfrak{q}_n.\]
By (L2), these intersections eventually stabilize. If the construction continued from such an $n$ to $n+1$, then $\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{q}_1\cap\cdots\cap\mathfrak{q}_n\cap\mathfrak{a}_{n+1}$, contradicting the maximality of $\mathfrak{a}_n$. Thus some $\mathfrak{a}_n=A$, giving a finite primary decomposition.
