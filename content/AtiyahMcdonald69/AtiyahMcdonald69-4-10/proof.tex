\textbf{(i)} If $a\in S_{\mathfrak{p}}(0)$, then $sa=0$ for some $s\notin\mathfrak{p}$, so $a\in\mathfrak{p}$.

\textbf{(ii)} If $\mathfrak{p}$ is minimal, $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ has just one prime ideal, so its nilradical is $\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}$. Contracting gives $r(S_{\mathfrak{p}}(0))=\mathfrak{p}$. Conversely, every prime $\mathfrak{p}'\subseteq\mathfrak{p}$ contains $S_{\mathfrak{p}}(0)$ by the argument in (i). If its radical is $\mathfrak{p}$, then $\mathfrak{p}'=\mathfrak{p}$.

\textbf{(iii)} An element outside $\mathfrak{p}$ is outside $\mathfrak{p}'$, so any annihilator witnessing membership in $S_{\mathfrak{p}}(0)$ also witnesses membership in $S_{\mathfrak{p}'}(0)$.

\textbf{(iv)} If $a\ne0$, choose a prime $\mathfrak{p}$ minimal over $(0:a)$. Then $\mathfrak{p}\in D(A)$, and $a\notin S_{\mathfrak{p}}(0)$ since every annihilator of $a$ lies in $\mathfrak{p}$.
