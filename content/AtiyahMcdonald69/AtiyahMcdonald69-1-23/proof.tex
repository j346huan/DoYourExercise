\textbf{(i)} Every prime contains exactly one of $f,1-f$, so $D(f)=V(1-f)$ is clopen.

\textbf{(ii)} Since each prime quotient is $\mathbb F_2$ by \exref{1.11}, a prime omits $1-\prod_i(1-f_i)$ iff it omits some $f_i$. Thus $\bigcup_iD(f_i)=D(1-\prod_i(1-f_i))$ for a finite family.

\textbf{(iii)} A clopen subset is quasi-compact, being closed in the quasi-compact spectrum. Its cover by basic opens has a finite subcover, which is a single basic open by (ii).

\textbf{(iv)} Distinct primes are distinct maximal ideals. Choose $f$ in one but not the other. The disjoint open sets $D(f),D(1-f)$ separate them. Hence the spectrum is Hausdorff, and its quasi-compactness makes it compact.
