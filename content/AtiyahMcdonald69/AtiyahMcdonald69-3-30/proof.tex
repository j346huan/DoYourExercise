If the topologies coincide, the Zariski spectrum is Hausdorff by \exref{3.28}, so $A/\mathfrak N$ is absolutely flat by \exref{3.11}.

Conversely, absolute flatness of $A/\mathfrak N$ makes the Zariski spectrum Hausdorff by \exref{3.11}. Each $D(f)$ is quasi-compact, hence closed, and is therefore clopen. By \exref{3.28}, the constructible topology is the smallest topology making all $D(f)$ clopen. It is thus contained in the Zariski topology, and the reverse inclusion always holds.
