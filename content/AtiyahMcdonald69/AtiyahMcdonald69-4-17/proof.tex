First let $\mathfrak{b}$ be a proper irreducible ideal and choose a prime $\mathfrak{p}$ minimal over it. By \exref{4.11} applied to $A/\mathfrak{b}$, $\mathfrak{q}=S_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{b})$ is $\mathfrak{p}$-primary. By hypothesis, $\mathfrak{q}=(\mathfrak{b}:x)$ for some $x\notin\mathfrak{p}$. We have
\[\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{q}\cap(\mathfrak{b}+(x)).\]
Indeed, if $b+ax\in\mathfrak{q}$ with $b\in\mathfrak{b}$, then $ax\in\mathfrak{q}$. Since $x\notin\mathfrak{p}$, primaryness gives $a\in\mathfrak{q}=(\mathfrak{b}:x)$, hence $ax\in\mathfrak{b}$. As $x\notin\mathfrak{b}$, irreducibility forces $\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{q}$, so $\mathfrak{b}$ is primary.

For each $y\notin\mathfrak{a}$, Zorn's lemma gives an ideal maximal among those containing $\mathfrak{a}$ and not containing $y$. It is irreducible: if it were the intersection of two strictly larger ideals, both would contain $y$. Thus it is primary. Intersecting these ideals over all $y\notin\mathfrak{a}$ gives $\mathfrak{a}$.
