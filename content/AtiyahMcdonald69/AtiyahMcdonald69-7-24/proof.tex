If $f^*$ is open, $f$ has going-down by \exref{5.10}(ii).

Conversely, suppose $f$ has going-down. Its image $E$ is constructible by \exref{7.23} and is closed under passage to smaller primes. If $E$ meets an irreducible closed set $V(\mathfrak{p})$, it therefore contains its generic point $\mathfrak{p}$. Thus $E\cap V(\mathfrak{p})$ is dense, and by \exref{7.20} it contains a nonempty open subset of $V(\mathfrak{p})$. Exercise \exref{7.22} shows that $E$ is open.

The same argument applies to $A\to B_g$ for every $g\in B$. This map is of finite type and retains going-down: a prime below a prime avoiding $g$ also avoids $g$. Hence the image of every basic open $D(g)$ is open. Arbitrary opens are unions of basic opens, so $f^*$ is open.
