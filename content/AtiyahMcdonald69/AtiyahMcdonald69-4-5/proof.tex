We have $\mathfrak{p}_1\cap\mathfrak{p}_2=(x,yz)$, since modulo $(x)$ the ideals $(y)$ and $(z)$ intersect in $(yz)$. Intersecting with $\mathfrak{m}^2$ removes only the possible linear term in $x$, so
\[\mathfrak{p}_1\cap\mathfrak{p}_2\cap\mathfrak{m}^2=(x^2,xy,xz,yz)=\mathfrak{p}_1\mathfrak{p}_2.\]
The first two components are prime, and $\mathfrak{m}^2$ is $\mathfrak{m}$-primary. Their radicals are distinct. The elements $z^2$, $y^2$ and $x$ respectively belong to the other two components but not to $\mathfrak{p}_1$, $\mathfrak{p}_2$ and $\mathfrak{m}^2$, so the decomposition is irredundant. The components $\mathfrak{p}_1,\mathfrak{p}_2$ are isolated, and $\mathfrak{m}^2$ is embedded.
