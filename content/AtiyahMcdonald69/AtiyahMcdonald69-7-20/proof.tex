\textbf{(i)} Each $U\cap C$ lies in $\mathcal{F}$, and finite unions do too by De Morgan's law. Conversely, finite unions of these intersections contain all open sets and are closed under finite intersections. The complement of one term is $(X\setminus U)\cup(X\setminus C)$, a union of two such terms. Distributing finite intersections over finite unions proves closure under complements, giving the assertion.

\textbf{(ii)} Every nonempty open subset of an irreducible space is dense. Conversely, write $E=\bigcup_{i=1}^n(U_i\cap C_i)$ and suppose $\overline{E}=X$. Then $X=\bigcup_i\overline{U_i\cap C_i}$, so irreducibility gives $\overline{U_i\cap C_i}=X$ for some $i$. As $C_i$ is closed, $C_i=X$, and $U_i$ is a nonempty open subset of $E$.
