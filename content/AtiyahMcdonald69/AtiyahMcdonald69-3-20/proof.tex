\textbf{(i)} A prime $\mathfrak p$ is contracted from an ideal exactly when $\mathfrak p^{ec}=\mathfrak p$. By Proposition 3.16, this is equivalent to its being the contraction of a prime ideal of $B$, which proves the assertion.

\textbf{(ii)} If $\mathfrak q=\mathfrak a^e$, then $\mathfrak a\subseteq\mathfrak q^c$ gives $\mathfrak q\subseteq\mathfrak q^{ce}\subseteq\mathfrak q$. Thus $\mathfrak q=\mathfrak q^{ce}$, so a prime of $B$ is determined by its contraction.

The converse fails for $k\to k[x]/(x^2)$. Both spectra have one point, so the induced map is injective, but the prime ideal $(x)$ is not extended: the only extensions from $k$ are $(0)$ and the whole ring.
