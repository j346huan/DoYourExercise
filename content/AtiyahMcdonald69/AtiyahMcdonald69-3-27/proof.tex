\textbf{(i)} The infinite tensor product is the directed limit of the finite tensor products $B_J=\bigotimes_{\alpha\in J}B_\alpha$. By \exref{3.25} and \exref{3.26},
\[f^*(\operatorname{Spec}(B))=\bigcap_J\bigcap_{\alpha\in J}f_\alpha^*(\operatorname{Spec}(B_\alpha))=\bigcap_\alpha f_\alpha^*(\operatorname{Spec}(B_\alpha)).\]

\textbf{(ii)} A finite product is a finite direct sum as an $A$-module, so $B\otimes_A k(\mathfrak p)\cong\bigoplus_\alpha(B_\alpha\otimes_A k(\mathfrak p))$. This is nonzero exactly when one summand is nonzero. The fiber criterion in \exref{3.21} gives the assertion.

\textbf{(iii)} Parts (i) and (ii) give closure under arbitrary intersections and finite unions. The maps $A\to A$ and $A\to0$ give the whole space and the empty set. Moreover, $V(\mathfrak a)$ is the image of $\operatorname{Spec}(A/\mathfrak a)$, so every Zariski closed set is constructibly closed.

\textbf{(iv)} Suppose a family of constructibly closed sets has empty intersection, and represent them as $f_\alpha^*(\operatorname{Spec}(B_\alpha))$. By (i), the infinite tensor product $B$ has empty spectrum, so $B=0$. Since $B=\varinjlim_J B_J$, the equality $1=0$ holds in some finite tensor product $B_J$. Applying (i) to this finite set gives a finite subfamily with empty intersection. Thus the constructible topology is quasi-compact.
