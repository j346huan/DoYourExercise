Suppose $A$ is Jacobson and $B$ is such a field. Replacing $A$ by its image in $B$ preserves the Jacobson property and does not change finite generation as a module. Thus assume $A\subseteq B$. Then $A$ is a domain with $J(A)=0$. Choose $s\neq0$ as in \exref{5.21}, and a maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ avoiding $s$. The map $A\to A/\mathfrak{m}$ extends to $B\to\overline{A/\mathfrak{m}}$. Since $B$ is a field, this map is injective, so $\mathfrak{m}=0$. Hence $A$ is a field and $B$ is algebraic over $A$. Its finitely many algebra generators are algebraic, so $B$ is finite over $A$.

Conversely, let $\mathfrak{p}$ be a nonmaximal prime and put $D=A/\mathfrak{p}$. For $0\neq f\in D$, the domain $D_f$ is a finitely generated $A$-algebra. If it were a field, it would be finite over $A$, hence finite and integral over $D$. Proposition 5.7 would then make $D$ a field, a contradiction. Thus $D_f$ has a nonzero maximal ideal. Its contraction is a nonzero prime of $D$ avoiding $f$. Consequently every element outside $\mathfrak{p}$ is avoided by some prime strictly containing $\mathfrak{p}$. Apply \exref{5.23}(iii).
