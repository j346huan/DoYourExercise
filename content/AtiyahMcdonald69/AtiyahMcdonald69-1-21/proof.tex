\textbf{(i)} A prime $\mathfrak q$ lies over $D(f)$ iff $f\notin\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak q)$, iff $\varphi(f)\notin\mathfrak q$. Thus $(\varphi^*)^{-1}(D(f))=D(\varphi(f))$, proving continuity.

\textbf{(ii)} The condition $\mathfrak a\subseteq\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak q)$ is equivalent to $\mathfrak a^e\subseteq\mathfrak q$.

\textbf{(iii)} The closure of a set of primes is $V$ of their intersection. Here
\[\bigcap_{\mathfrak q\supseteq\mathfrak b}\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak q)=\varphi^{-1}(\sqrt{\mathfrak b})=\sqrt{\mathfrak b^c}.\]
Hence $\overline{\varphi^*(V(\mathfrak b))}=V(\mathfrak b^c)$.

\textbf{(iv)} For surjective $\varphi$, the prime correspondence gives a bijection onto $V(\ker\varphi)$. Moreover $\varphi^*(V(\mathfrak b))=V(\mathfrak b^c)$ by the same correspondence, so the map is closed and is a homeomorphism. Apply this to $A\to A/\sqrt{(0)}$ for the stated special case.

\textbf{(v)} Taking $\mathfrak b=0$ in (iii) gives $\overline{\varphi^*(\operatorname{Spec}B)}=V(\ker\varphi)$. This is the whole spectrum iff $\ker\varphi\subseteq\sqrt{(0)}$; in particular, injectivity suffices.

\textbf{(vi)} Inverse images satisfy $(\psi\circ\varphi)^{-1}=\varphi^{-1}\circ\psi^{-1}$.

\textbf{(vii)} Both $A/\mathfrak p$ and $K$ are fields. The two primes of their product contract to $\mathfrak p$ and $(0)$, respectively. Thus the spectral map is bijective. Its domain is discrete, whereas $\overline{\{(0)\}}=\operatorname{Spec}A$, so it is not a homeomorphism.
