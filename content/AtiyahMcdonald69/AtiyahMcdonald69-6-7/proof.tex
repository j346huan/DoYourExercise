If some closed subset is not a finite union of irreducible closed subsets, choose a minimal such subset. It is nonempty and reducible, so it is the union of two proper closed subsets. By minimality, each is a finite union of irreducible closed subsets, a contradiction.

Write $X$ as a finite union of irreducible closed subsets. Every irreducible component is contained in one of them, since an irreducible space contained in a finite union of closed sets is contained in one member. Maximality then makes the component equal to that member. Hence there are only finitely many components.
