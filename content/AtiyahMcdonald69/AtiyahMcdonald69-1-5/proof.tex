\textbf{(i)} If $fg=1$, comparison of constant terms makes $a_0$ a unit. Conversely, write $f=a_0(1-h)$ with $h\in xA[[x]]$. Then $a_0^{-1}\sum_{n\geq0}h^n$ is an inverse; each coefficient involves only finitely many summands.

\textbf{(ii)} For every prime ideal $\mathfrak p\subset A$, the image of $f$ in the domain $(A/\mathfrak p)[[x]]$ is zero. Hence every $a_n$ belongs to every prime ideal and is nilpotent. The converse fails for $A=\mathbb Q[t_1,t_2,\ldots]/(t_1^2,t_2^2,\ldots)$ and $f=\sum_{i\geq1}t_ix^i$. In $f^n$, the coefficient of $x^{n(n+1)/2}$ contains the nonzero monomial $n!t_1\cdots t_n$, which cannot cancel. Thus $f^n\ne0$ for every $n$.

\textbf{(iii)} By (i), $1+fg$ is a unit iff $1+a_0b_0$ is a unit, where $b_0$ is the constant term of $g$. Letting $g$ range over all series is equivalent to letting $b_0$ range over $A$.

\textbf{(iv)} By (iii), $x$ belongs to every maximal ideal. The quotient $A[[x]]/(x)\cong A$ therefore identifies maximal ideals with those of $A$. The inverse image of $\mathfrak m$ is $\mathfrak m A[[x]]+(x)$, and its contraction is $\mathfrak m$.

\textbf{(v)} The inverse image of a prime ideal under the constant-term map $A[[x]]\to A$ is prime and has the required contraction.
