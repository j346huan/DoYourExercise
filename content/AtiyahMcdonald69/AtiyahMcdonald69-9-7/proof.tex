For a proper nonzero ideal, factor $\mathfrak a=\prod_i\mathfrak p_i^{e_i}$. The Chinese Remainder Theorem and localization give
\[A/\mathfrak a\cong\prod_i A/\mathfrak p_i^{e_i}\cong\prod_i A_{\mathfrak p_i}/\mathfrak p_i^{e_i}A_{\mathfrak p_i}.\]
Each factor is a quotient of a principal ideal domain, so all its ideals are principal. An ideal in a finite product is the product of its component ideals; the tuple of their generators generates it. Hence every ideal of $A/\mathfrak a$ is principal. The assertion also holds when $\mathfrak a=A$.

For any nonzero ideal $\mathfrak b$, choose $0\ne a\in\mathfrak b$. The ideal $\mathfrak b/(a)$ of $A/(a)$ is principal. Lifting a generator to $b\in\mathfrak b$ gives $\mathfrak b=(a,b)$. The zero ideal needs only the generator $0$.
