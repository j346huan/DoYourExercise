Flatness implies the stated vanishing by \exref{2.24}. Conversely, the Tor exact sequence for $0\to\mathfrak{a}\to A\to A/\mathfrak{a}\to0$ identifies $\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(A/\mathfrak{a},N)$ with the kernel of $\mathfrak{a}\otimes_A N\to A\otimes_A N$. Thus this map is injective for every finitely generated ideal.

For an arbitrary ideal $\mathfrak{a}$, any element of that kernel is represented by a finite sum $\sum_i a_i\otimes n_i$. It comes from $(a_1,\ldots,a_r)\otimes_A N$ and maps to zero in $A\otimes_A N$. Injectivity for this finitely generated ideal makes the element zero. Hence $\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(A/\mathfrak{a},N)=0$ for every ideal, so Tor vanishes for every cyclic module.

If $M$ is generated by $x_1,\ldots,x_r$, put $M_i=Ax_1+\cdots+Ax_i$ and $M_0=0$. Each $M_i/M_{i-1}$ is cyclic. The exact segment $\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(M_{i-1},N)\to\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(M_i,N)\to\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(M_i/M_{i-1},N)$ proves by induction that $\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(M,N)=0$.

For an injection $L\to M$ of finitely generated modules, the quotient is finitely generated, so its vanishing Tor group makes $L\otimes_A N\to M\otimes_A N$ injective. Proposition 2.19(iv) now gives flatness of $N$.
