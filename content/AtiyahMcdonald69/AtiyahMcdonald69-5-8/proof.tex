(i) Choose a field containing $B$ in which $f$ and $g$ split. Every root is integral over $C$, since it is a root of the monic polynomial $fg$. The coefficients of $f$ and $g$ are sums of products of these roots, so they are integral over $C$, hence over $A$. They belong to $B$, and therefore to $C$.

(ii) A positive-degree monic polynomial $h\in B[x]$ acquires a root in $B[x]/(h)$, and $B\to B[x]/(h)$ is injective: no nonzero constant is a multiple of a positive-degree monic polynomial. Dividing by the corresponding monic linear factor and repeating embeds $B$ in a ring in which both $f$ and $g$ split. Each root is integral over $C$, and the argument in (i) applies unchanged.
