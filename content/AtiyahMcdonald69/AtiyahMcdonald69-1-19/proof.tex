If the nilradical is prime, the spectrum is nonempty. By \exref{1.17}, nonempty basic opens $D(f),D(g)$ correspond to nonnilpotent $f,g$, and their intersection is $D(fg)$. Primality of the nilradical makes this intersection nonempty, so the spectrum is irreducible.

Conversely, irreducibility implies that the spectrum is nonempty and that the product of two nonnilpotent elements is nonnilpotent, again by \exref{1.17}. Thus the nilradical is a proper prime ideal.
