By \exref{7.20}, it suffices to consider a locally closed subset $U\cap C$. Quotienting $B$ by an ideal defining $C$ reduces to an open subset. Since $\operatorname{Spec}(B)$ is Noetherian, this open subset is a finite union of basic opens $D(g)=\operatorname{Spec}(B_g)$. Each $B_g$ is still of finite type over $A$, so it suffices to show that the whole image is constructible.

Apply \exref{7.21}. Let $X_0=V(\mathfrak{p})$ be irreducible closed and suppose the image meets it densely. Replacing $A,B$ by $A/\mathfrak{p},B/\mathfrak{p}B$ reduces to the case where $A$ is a domain and the image is dense. Density forces the kernel to lie in the nilradical of $A$, so the map is injective. The spectrum of $B$ has finitely many irreducible components. Since their images have dense union in an irreducible space, one has dense image. Quotienting by its defining prime reduces further to an injection of domains $A\subseteq B$ of finite type.

By \exref{5.21}, there is $0\ne s\in A$ such that every homomorphism from $A$ to an algebraically closed field which does not kill $s$ extends to $B$. For $\mathfrak{p}\in D(s)$, apply this to $A\to\overline{k(\mathfrak{p})}$. The kernel of the extension is a prime of $B$ contracting to $\mathfrak{p}$. Thus the image contains the nonempty open set $D(s)$, proving the required criterion.
