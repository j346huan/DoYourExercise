\textbf{(i)$\Rightarrow$(ii)} The unique prime is both the nilradical and the unique maximal ideal. Every element outside it is a unit.

\textbf{(ii)$\Rightarrow$(iii)} Nilpotent elements cannot be units since $A\ne0$. Thus the nonunits are exactly $\mathfrak n$, so $\mathfrak n$ is the unique maximal ideal.

\textbf{(iii)$\Rightarrow$(i)} Every prime contains $\mathfrak n$, and the field $A/\mathfrak n$ has only the zero prime.
