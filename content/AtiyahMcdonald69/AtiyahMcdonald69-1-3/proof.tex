Write $f=\sum_i g_ix_r^i$ with $g_i\in A[x_1,\ldots,x_{r-1}]$. By \exref{1.2}(i), $f$ is a unit iff $g_0$ is a unit and every $g_i$, $i>0$, is nilpotent. Induction on $r$, using \exref{1.2}(ii) at each step, gives exactly the stated unit and nilpotence criteria.

Suppose $fg=0$ with $g\ne0$. Choose $N$ larger than every exponent occurring in $f$ or $g$, and substitute $x_i=t^{N^{i-1}}$. Distinct monomials of either polynomial remain distinct, so the resulting polynomials have zero product and the second is nonzero. By \exref{1.2}(iii), some nonzero $a\in A$ kills every coefficient of the first. These are exactly the coefficients of $f$, so $af=0$. The converse is immediate.

For primitivity, reduction modulo any maximal ideal gives a polynomial ring over a field, hence a domain. The argument of \exref{1.2}(iv) applies without change.
