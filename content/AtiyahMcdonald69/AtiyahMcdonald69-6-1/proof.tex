\textbf{(i)} The ascending chain $\ker u\subseteq\ker u^2\subseteq\cdots$ stabilizes, say $\ker u^n=\ker u^{n+1}$. If $x\in\ker u$, choose $y$ with $u^ny=x$ by surjectivity. Then $u^{n+1}y=0$, so $y\in\ker u^n$ and $x=0$.

\textbf{(ii)} The descending chain $u(M)\supseteq u^2(M)\supseteq\cdots$ stabilizes, say $u^n(M)=u^{n+1}(M)$. For $x\in M$, write $u^nx=u^{n+1}y$. Injectivity of $u^n$ gives $x=uy$, so $u$ is surjective.
