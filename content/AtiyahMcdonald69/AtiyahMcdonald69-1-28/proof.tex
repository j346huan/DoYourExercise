A regular map pulls polynomial functions back to polynomial functions, so it induces a $k$-algebra homomorphism.

Conversely, let $\varphi:P(Y)\to P(X)$ be a $k$-algebra homomorphism and let $y_1,\ldots,y_m$ be the coordinate functions on $Y$. Define $f(x)=(\varphi(y_1)(x),\ldots,\varphi(y_m)(x))$. If $g\in I(Y)$, then $g(f(x))=\varphi(g)(x)=0$, so $f(x)\in Y$. Choosing polynomial representatives of the $\varphi(y_i)$ makes $f$ regular. For every polynomial function $g$ on $Y$, its pullback is $g(f(x))=\varphi(g)(x)$. Since the coordinate functions generate $P(Y)$, the two constructions are inverse.
