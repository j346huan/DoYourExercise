Suppose $b\notin A$ is integral, and choose a monic equation $b^n+a_1b^{n-1}+\cdots+a_n=0$ of least degree. Degree one would give $b\in A$, so $n>1$. The product $b(b^{n-1}+a_1b^{n-2}+\cdots+a_{n-1})=-a_n$ lies in $A$. Since $b\notin A$, multiplicative closure of the complement implies that the second factor lies in $A$. Subtracting its value gives a monic equation for $b$ of degree $n-1$, a contradiction.
