(a) A finite-type morphism is locally of finite type, and the finite affine covers in its definition make it quasi-compact. Conversely, for a locally finite-type, quasi-compact morphism, each inverse image of an affine open is quasi-compact and therefore admits a finite subcover of a cover by affines of finite type.

(b) Apply \exref{II.3.1} and \exref{II.3.2} to (a).

(c) Cover $f^{-1}(V)$ by affines $\operatorname{Spec}A_j$ with $A_j$ finitely generated over $B$. Using common distinguished neighborhoods as in \exref{II.3.1}, the affine $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$ has a finite distinguished cover $D(g_i)$ such that each $A_{g_i}$ is a localization of some $A_j$ at one element, hence is finitely generated over $B$.

Choose $c_i\in A$ with $\sum_i c_ig_i=1$, and let $C\subseteq A$ be the $B$-subalgebra generated by the $c_i$, the $g_i$, and numerators of finite generating sets for all the $A_{g_i}$. For any $a\in A$, equality in each localization implies $g_i^Na\in C$ for a sufficiently large common $N$. Since the $g_i$ generate the unit ideal of $C$, so do their $N$th powers. Thus $a\in C$, proving that $A$ is finitely generated over $B$.
