Suppose $f$ is finite, and let $V=\operatorname{Spec}B\subseteq Y$ be affine. By common distinguished neighborhoods as in \exref{II.3.1}, there is a finite distinguished cover $D(b_i)$ of $V$ such that each $f^{-1}(D(b_i))$ is affine and finite over $D(b_i)$: finiteness of modules is preserved by localization.

Put $U=f^{-1}(V)$ and let $a_i\in\Gamma(U,\mathcal O_U)$ be the pullbacks of $b_i$. Locality gives $U_{a_i}=f^{-1}(D(b_i))$, and the $a_i$ generate the unit ideal. By \exref{II.2.17}, $U$ is affine, say $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$. Thus $A_{b_i}$ is finite over $B_{b_i}$ for every $i$.

Choose finitely many elements of $A$ whose images generate all the $A_{b_i}$, and let $M$ be their $B$-span. For every $a\in A$, its class in $A/M$ vanishes after localization at each $b_i$, so some common powers $b_i^N$ kill it. These powers generate the unit ideal of $B$, hence $a\in M$. Thus $A$ is finite over $B$. The converse is immediate.
