(a) By (b), a closed immersion is locally $\operatorname{Spec}(A/I)\to\operatorname{Spec}A$. After affine base change $A\to A'$, it becomes $\operatorname{Spec}(A'/IA')\to\operatorname{Spec}A'$, again a closed immersion. These affine verifications cover the base change.

(b) Identify $Y$ with its closed image in $X$. Given $y\in Y$, choose an affine neighborhood $W\subseteq Y$ and $f\in A$ with $y\in D(f)$ and $D(f)\cap Y\subseteq W$. Then $D(f)\cap Y=W_{f|_W}$, which is affine. Since $Y$ is quasi-compact, finitely many such opens cover it. Add distinguished opens covering $X\setminus Y$, and use quasi-compactness of $X$ to retain finitely many $D(f_i)$ covering $X$. The $f_i$ generate the unit ideal of $A$, so their images generate the unit ideal of $\Gamma(Y,\mathcal O_Y)$. All the corresponding $Y_{f_i}$ are affine, including the empty ones. By \exref{II.2.17}, $Y$ is affine. Now \exref{II.2.18}(d) makes $A\to\Gamma(Y,\mathcal O_Y)$ surjective, so $Y=\operatorname{Spec}(A/I)$ for its kernel $I$.

(c) On $\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq X$, write $Y'=\operatorname{Spec}(A/I)$. Its reduced induced subscheme is $\operatorname{Spec}(A/\sqrt I)$, and $A\to A/\sqrt I$ factors through $A/I$. These factorizations commute with restriction, so glue.

(d) For every closed subscheme through which $f$ factors, take its defining ideals on affine opens of $X$, and sum these ideals. By (b), their restrictions to distinguished opens are the corresponding localizations. Localization commutes with sums, so the sums define a closed subscheme $Y\subseteq X$. The pullbacks of all summands vanish on $Z$, hence so do their sums; therefore $f$ factors through $Y$. The sum contains each defining ideal, so $Y$ factors through every other such closed subscheme. This also proves uniqueness.

If $Z$ is reduced, a section vanishing on $\overline{f(Z)}$ pulls back to a section lying in every prime on each affine open of $Z$, hence to zero. Thus $f$ factors through the reduced induced structure on $\overline{f(Z)}$. Every closed subscheme through which $f$ factors has support containing $\overline{f(Z)}$, and its defining ideal is contained in the radical ideal defining that reduced structure. Hence this is the scheme-theoretic image.
