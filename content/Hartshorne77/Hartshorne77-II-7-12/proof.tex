Let $\mathcal I$ and $\mathcal J$ define $Y$ and $Z$, and write $\mathcal S=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}(\mathcal I+\mathcal J)^n$. In $\operatorname{Proj}\mathcal S$, consider the closed sets cut out by $\mathcal I\subseteq\mathcal S_1$ and $\mathcal J\subseteq\mathcal S_1$.

The inverse image of $Y-(Y\cap Z)$ lies in the first closed set. Indeed, near such a point $\mathcal I+\mathcal J=\mathcal O_X$, so the blowup is the identity and its degree-one sections from $\mathcal I$ vanish on $Y$. Taking closures shows that the strict transform of $Y$ lies in this closed set. Similarly, the strict transform of $Z$ lies in the second. Their intersection is empty, since together their equations generate $\mathcal S_1$, and no point of $\operatorname{Proj}\mathcal S$ contains the irrelevant ideal.
