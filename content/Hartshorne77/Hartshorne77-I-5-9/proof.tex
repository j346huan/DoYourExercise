If $f$ has a repeated nonconstant factor $g$, then every partial derivative of $f$ vanishes on $Z(g)$, contrary to the hypothesis. Otherwise, a nontrivial factorization $f=gh$ has relatively prime homogeneous factors of positive degree. By \exref{I.3.7}, $Z(g)\cap Z(h)$ is nonempty; at any point of this intersection all partial derivatives of $f$ vanish. Hence $f$ is irreducible, and \exref{I.5.8} gives nonsingularity.
