A section of the inverse-limit presheaf is a compatible tuple $(s_i)$. Uniqueness of gluing holds in each coordinate. Given compatible tuples on an open cover, glue their $i$th coordinates in $\mathscr F_i$. The resulting sections satisfy the transition relations because those relations hold locally and sections are determined locally. Thus the presheaf is a sheaf.

Given compatible morphisms $\mathscr G\to\mathscr F_i$, sending a section to the tuple of its images gives a morphism $\mathscr G\to\varprojlim_i\mathscr F_i$. Its coordinate projections are the given morphisms, and these determine it uniquely. Hence it has the universal property of the inverse limit.
