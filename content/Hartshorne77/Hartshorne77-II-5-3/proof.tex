Given an $A$-linear map $u:M\to\Gamma(X,\mathcal F)$, define $M_f\to\mathcal F(D(f))$ by $m/f^n\mapsto f^{-n}u(m)|_{D(f)}$. This is well-defined because $f$ is invertible on $D(f)$. The maps commute with restrictions: both restriction maps are the unique extensions of $m\mapsto u(m)$ after the required functions become invertible. They therefore define $\widetilde M\to\mathcal F$.

Conversely, taking global sections gives $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_X}(\widetilde M,\mathcal F)\to\operatorname{Hom}_A(M,\Gamma(X,\mathcal F))$. The two constructions are inverse, since an $\mathcal O_X$-linear map on $D(f)$ must send $m/f^n$ to $f^{-n}$ times the image of $m$. They are natural in both arguments.
