(a) For $m\ge n$, the kernel of $\Gamma(Y_m,\mathcal O_{Y_m})\to\Gamma(Y_n,\mathcal O_{Y_n})$ is nilpotent. A surjective ring map with nilpotent kernel is surjective on units: lifts $a,b$ of mutually inverse elements satisfy $ab=1+c$ with $c$ nilpotent, so $a$ is invertible. Therefore the image of the unit group is exactly the unit group of the image ring. For fixed $n$, these image rings stabilize by hypothesis, so their unit groups stabilize as well.

(b) Let $T_n$ be the set of isomorphisms $\mathcal F/\mathcal J^n\mathcal F\to\mathcal O_{Y_n}$. It is a nonempty torsor under $\Gamma(Y_n,\mathcal O_{Y_n}^*)$. For fixed $n$, the images of $T_m$ in $T_n$ are nested nonempty cosets of the corresponding images of unit groups. By (a), their subgroups eventually agree, so the cosets eventually agree. Thus $(T_n)$ satisfies (ML), and the successive-choice argument for (9.1) gives compatible isomorphisms. Their inverse limit is $\mathcal F\cong\mathcal O_{\mathfrak X}$ by (9.6). Applying this to the quotient of two invertible sheaves proves injectivity.

(c) Choose the given isomorphisms for consecutive indices and compose the resulting quotient maps. The resulting inverse system satisfies (9.6(b)), so $\mathcal L=\varprojlim_n\mathcal L_n$ is coherent and $\mathcal L/\mathcal J^n\mathcal L\cong\mathcal L_n$. Fix an affine formal open subset $U$ on which $\mathcal L_1$ is free. Each $Y_n|_U$ is affine. By (5.6), a basis of $\mathcal L_n|_U$ lifts to a section of $\mathcal L_{n+1}|_U$. This lift is a basis: locally its coefficient in any basis is a unit modulo a nilpotent ideal, hence a unit. Starting with a basis of $\mathcal L_1|_U$, choose compatible bases for every $n$. Taking their inverse limit gives $\mathcal L|_U\cong\mathcal O_U$. This proves surjectivity.

(d) If $\mathfrak X$ is affine, the maps on global sections of $\mathcal O_{Y_n}$ are surjective by (5.6). If each $Y_n$ is projective over $k$, these spaces are finite-dimensional over $k$ by (5.19), so the descending chains of images stabilize.
