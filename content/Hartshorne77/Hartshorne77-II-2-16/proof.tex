(a) For $x=\mathfrak p\in U=\operatorname{Spec}B$, the germ of $f$ is the image of $\bar f$ in $B_{\mathfrak p}$. It lies outside $\mathfrak pB_{\mathfrak p}$ exactly when $\bar f\notin\mathfrak p$. Thus $U\cap X_f=D(\bar f)$, and $X_f$ is open.

(b) Choose a finite affine open cover $U_i=\operatorname{Spec}B_i$. Since $a|_{U_i}$ vanishes in $(B_i)_{\bar f}$, some power of $\bar f$ kills it. Taking the maximum of these finitely many exponents gives $f^na=0$ on every $U_i$, hence on $X$.

(c) After choosing one common exponent $n$, the restrictions of $f^nb$ extend to sections $b_i\in\mathcal O_X(U_i)$. On $U_i\cap U_j$, the section $b_i-b_j$ vanishes after restriction to $X_f$. By (b), applied to the quasi-compact intersection, a power of $f$ kills this difference. Taking a common exponent $m$ for all pairs, the sections $f^mb_i$ agree and glue to an element of $A$ restricting to $f^{n+m}b$.

(d) The restriction of $f$ to $X_f$ is a unit, so restriction induces $A_f\to\Gamma(X_f,\mathcal O_{X_f})$. It is injective by (b) and surjective by (c).
