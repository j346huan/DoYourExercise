Write $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$. Since $\mathcal J^2=0$, $X'$ and $X$ have the same underlying topological space. By (III, 3.5), $H^1(X,\mathcal F)=0$. Taking sections of $0\to\mathcal J\to\mathcal O_{X'}\to\mathcal O_X\to0$ therefore gives a square-zero extension
\[0\to\Gamma(X,\mathcal F)\to\Gamma(X',\mathcal O_{X'})\to A\to0.\]
By \exref{II.8.6}, the last map has a $k$-algebra section. This section induces a morphism $X'\to\operatorname{Spec}A=X$ whose restriction to $X$ is the identity. On the common topological space, it gives a splitting $\mathcal O_X\to\mathcal O_{X'}$ of the quotient map. Consequently $\mathcal O_{X'}\cong\mathcal O_X\oplus\mathcal F$, and multiplication is $(a,u)(b,v)=(ab,av+bu)$ because $\mathcal J^2=0$. Thus the extension is trivial.
