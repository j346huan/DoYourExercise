(a) By \exref{II.3.11}(b), a closed immersion is locally induced by $A\to A/I$. A quotient is a finitely generated $A$-algebra, so the morphism is of finite type.

(b) An open immersion is locally of finite type: inside an affine open $\operatorname{Spec}A$, its image is covered by distinguished opens with rings $A_f=A[1/f]$. Quasi-compactness and \exref{II.3.3}(a) give finite type.

(c) The inverse image of a quasi-compact open subset under a quasi-compact morphism is quasi-compact, as follows by taking a finite affine cover of that open subset. Thus compositions of quasi-compact morphisms are quasi-compact. On affine neighborhoods, finite generation is transitive: generators for an algebra over an intermediate algebra, together with generators for that intermediate algebra, generate over the base. By \exref{II.3.3}, the composition is of finite type.

(d) This can be checked on affine opens of the new base. An affine finite-type chart is given by $B\to A$ with $A$ finitely generated over $B$; after $B\to B'$, its ring is $A\otimes_BB'$, generated over $B'$ by the images of the same generators. The finite affine covers remain finite under base change, proving the assertion.

(e) The projection $X\times_SY\to Y$ is of finite type by (d), and its composition with $Y\to S$ is of finite type by (c).

(f) Choose affine neighborhoods $W\subseteq Z$, $V\subseteq g^{-1}(W)$, and $U\subseteq f^{-1}(V)$. By \exref{II.3.3}(c) applied to $g\circ f$, $\Gamma(U,\mathcal O_U)$ is finitely generated over $\Gamma(W,\mathcal O_W)$, hence over $\Gamma(V,\mathcal O_V)$, since the ring maps factor through it. These neighborhoods prove that $f$ is locally of finite type. Its assumed quasi-compactness and \exref{II.3.3}(a) finish the proof.

(g) A finite affine cover of the noetherian scheme $Y$ pulls back to finitely many affine charts whose coordinate rings are finitely generated over noetherian rings. They are noetherian by the Hilbert basis theorem, so $X$ is noetherian.
