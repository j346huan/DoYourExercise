Choose affine neighborhoods of $P$ and $Q$. An isomorphism $\mathcal O_{Q,Y}\to\mathcal O_{P,X}$ extends to an isomorphism of their fraction fields. The images of finitely many generators of $A(Y)$ are regular on a neighborhood $U$ of $P$, giving a morphism $\varphi:U\to Y$. The local isomorphism identifies maximal ideals and fixes $k$, so $\varphi(P)=Q$. Its inverse similarly gives $\psi:V\to X$ with $\psi(Q)=P$.

On $U\cap\varphi^{-1}(V)$ and $V\cap\psi^{-1}(U)$, the compositions are defined and equal to the identity, since they induce the identity on function fields. These two open neighborhoods are carried into each other by $\varphi$ and $\psi$, so their restrictions are inverse isomorphisms.
