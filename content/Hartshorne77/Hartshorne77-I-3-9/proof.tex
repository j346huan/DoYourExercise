By \exref{I.3.4}, $X\cong Y$, but $S(X)=k[s,t]$ and $S(Y)\cong k[s^2,st,t^2]$. The elements $s^2,st,t^2$ are irreducible in the latter ring: every nonconstant factor has degree at least $2$. They are pairwise nonassociate, and $s^2t^2=(st)^2$ gives two distinct factorizations. Thus $S(Y)$ is not a UFD, whereas $S(X)$ is, so the rings are not isomorphic.
