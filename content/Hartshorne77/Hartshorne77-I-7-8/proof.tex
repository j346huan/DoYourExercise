Choose a nonsingular point $P\in Y$, which exists by Theorem 5.3. By \exref{I.7.7}, the cone formed by the lines from $P$ to $Y$ has dimension $r+1$ and degree less than $2$, hence degree $1$. By \exref{I.7.6}, it is a linear subspace $L\cong\mathbb{P}^{r+1}$. Thus $Y\subset L$ is an irreducible hypersurface. Its defining homogeneous polynomial has degree $\deg Y=2$, so it is a quadric hypersurface.
