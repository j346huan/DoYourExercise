The points are $(0)$, the ideals $(x-a)$ for $a\in\mathbb R$, and the ideals $((x-a)^2+b^2)$ for $a\in\mathbb R$ and $b>0$. Every nonzero prime is maximal. The proper closed subsets are exactly the finite sets of these closed points, and $(0)$ is generic.

The closed points identify with $\mathbb C$ modulo complex conjugation, with the cofinite topology. The points coming from $\mathbb R$ form the subset corresponding to linear polynomials. Unlike $\mathbb R$ or $\mathbb C$ with either their usual or their affine Zariski topologies, $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb R[x]$ has a nonclosed generic point.
