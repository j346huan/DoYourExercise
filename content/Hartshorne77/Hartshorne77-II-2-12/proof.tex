Set $U_{ii}=X_i$ and $\varphi_{ii}=\operatorname{id}$. On $\coprod_iX_i$, declare $x\in X_i$ equivalent to $\varphi_{ij}(x)\in X_j$ whenever $x\in U_{ij}$. The inverse and cocycle conditions make this an equivalence relation. Give the quotient $X$ the quotient topology and denote the maps by $\psi_i$. They are injective, and for open $V\subseteq X_i$ we have $\psi_j^{-1}(\psi_i(V))=\varphi_{ij}(V\cap U_{ij})$, which is open. Thus each $\psi_i$ is a homeomorphism onto an open subset, and their images have the required intersections.

For open $V\subseteq X$, define $\mathcal O_X(V)$ to be the ring of families $s_i\in\mathcal O_{X_i}(\psi_i^{-1}(V))$ whose restrictions correspond under the $\varphi_{ij}^\#$. Restrictions act componentwise. The sheaf axioms follow from those of the $\mathcal O_{X_i}$, and restriction to $\psi_i(X_i)$ recovers $\mathcal O_{X_i}$. Thus $X$ has an affine open cover and is a scheme, with each $\psi_i$ an open immersion satisfying all the stated identities. When all overlaps are empty, this construction is the disjoint union.
