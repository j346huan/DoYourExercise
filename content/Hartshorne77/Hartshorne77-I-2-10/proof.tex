(a) $C(Y)=V(I(Y))$ in $\mathbb A^{n+1}$. If a polynomial vanishes on $C(Y)$, evaluating it at $tP$ for all $t\in k$ shows that each homogeneous component vanishes on $C(Y)$, hence on $Y$. Therefore its ideal is exactly $I(Y)$.

(b) By (a), $C(Y)$ is irreducible exactly when $I(Y)$ is prime, which is equivalent to irreducibility of $Y$ by \exref{I.2.4}.

(c) For irreducible $Y$, $\dim C(Y)=\dim S(Y)=\dim Y+1$ by \exref{I.2.6}. For an algebraic set, apply this to its finitely many irreducible components and take the maximum.
