Every nonempty open subset contains a nonempty affine open $\operatorname{Spec}A$, where $A$ is finitely generated over $k$ by \exref{II.3.3}(c). Choose a maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$ of $A$. Its residue field is finite over $k$ by Zariski's lemma.

The corresponding point is closed in $X$. Indeed, any specialization has an affine neighborhood containing it and the original point. On this affine neighborhood the prime of the original point has quotient a $k$-subalgebra of its finite residue field, hence a finite-dimensional domain over $k$ and therefore a field. The prime is maximal, so the specialization is the same point. Thus every nonempty open contains a closed point.

For $X=\operatorname{Spec}k[t]_{(t)}$, the only closed point is $(t)$, and it is not dense: the nonempty open $D(t)=\{(0)\}$ misses it.
