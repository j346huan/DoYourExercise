Two affine opens have a basis of common distinguished open neighborhoods on their intersection. Indeed, choose $D_U(a)\subseteq U\cap V$ around a given point, then choose $D_V(b)\subseteq D_U(a)$ around it. On $D_U(a)$ write $b=c/a^n$; then $D_V(b)=D_U(ac)$.

Suppose $V_i=\operatorname{Spec}B_i$ cover $Y$, and $f^{-1}(V_i)$ is covered by $\operatorname{Spec}A_{ij}$ with $A_{ij}$ finitely generated over $B_i$. For an arbitrary affine open $V=\operatorname{Spec}B$, cover $V$ by common distinguished opens $W=D_V(b)=D_{V_i}(c)$. The preimage of $W$ is covered by the distinguished opens $\operatorname{Spec}(A_{ij})_{\varphi_{ij}(c)}$, whose rings are finitely generated over $(B_i)_c\cong B_b$ and hence over $B$. These opens cover $f^{-1}(V)$. The converse follows directly from the definition.
