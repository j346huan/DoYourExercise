(a) Addition and the inverse $a\mapsto-a$ are polynomial maps, and $k$ is a group under addition. Thus $G_a$ is a group variety.

(b) Multiplication is polynomial, and $a\mapsto1/a$ is regular on $\mathbb A^1\setminus\{0\}$. Thus $G_m$ is a group variety.

(c) Set $(fg)(x)=\mu(f(x),g(x))$. This is a morphism by the universal property of products and composition. The inverse is $x\mapsto f(x)^{-1}$, also a morphism; the identity is the constant map to the identity of $G$. The group axioms hold pointwise.

(d) Morphisms $X\to\mathbb A^1$ are exactly regular functions on $X$, by (3.6). Pointwise group multiplication in $G_a$ is addition, giving $\operatorname{Hom}(X,G_a)\cong\mathcal O(X)$ as additive groups.

(e) Morphisms $X\to G_m$ are exactly nowhere-zero regular functions, whose reciprocals are regular. Thus $\operatorname{Hom}(X,G_m)\cong\mathcal O(X)^\times$ under multiplication.
