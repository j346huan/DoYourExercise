(a) Distinct irreducible components of a noetherian normal scheme cannot meet, since a local ring at an intersection would have more than one minimal prime. The components are therefore disjoint and open. Connectedness makes $X$ integral. Its saturated homogeneous ideal is prime: for homogeneous $f,g$, the equality $fg|_X=0$ and integrality imply that one factor vanishes identically; saturation then puts that factor in the ideal. Thus $S$ is a domain.

View $\mathcal A=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}\mathcal O_X(n)$ inside the constant sheaf with field $\operatorname{Frac}(S)$. Locally, a generator of $\mathcal O_X(1)$ identifies $\mathcal A_x$ with $\mathcal O_{X,x}[t]$, which is integrally closed. If an element of $\operatorname{Frac}(S)$ is integral over $S'$, it belongs to every $\mathcal A_x$, hence is a global section of $\mathcal A$. Thus $S'$ is integrally closed. Here $\Gamma(X,\mathcal A)=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}\Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X(n))$, since $X$ is quasi-compact. The proof of Theorem 5.19 shows that $S'$ is finite over $S$ inside $\operatorname{Frac}(S)$. Hence it is the integral closure of $S$.

(b) Apply \exref{II.5.9}(b) to $M=S$.

(c) Since $X$ is integral and projective, $S'_0=\Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X)=k=S_0$. Thus (b) gives $S^{(d)}=(S')^{(d)}$ for sufficiently large $d$. A Veronese subring of a normal graded domain is normal: writing $B=(S')^{(d)}$, an element of $\operatorname{Frac}(B)$ integral over $B$ lies in $S'$ by normality, and $S'\cap\operatorname{Frac}(B)=B$. For the latter equality, multiply by a nonzero denominator in $B$ and decompose by degrees modulo $d$; all nonzero residue-degree components must vanish. Therefore $S^{(d)}$ is normal, and \exref{II.5.13} proves the assertion.

(d) If $S$ is normal, each $S_{(x_i)}$ is normal: an element of its fraction field integral over it belongs to $S_{x_i}$ and has degree zero. Thus $X$ is normal, and (a) gives $S=S'$, so every restriction map is surjective. Conversely, surjectivity in degree zero gives $\Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X)=k$, hence $X$ is connected. If $X$ is normal, (a) applies, and surjectivity in every degree says exactly that $S=S'$. Thus $S$ is normal.
