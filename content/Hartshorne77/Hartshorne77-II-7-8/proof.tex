Apply (7.12) with $Y=X$ and $g=\operatorname{id}_X$. Explicitly, a section $\sigma$ gives the quotient $\mathcal E\to\sigma^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P(\mathcal E)}(1)$ obtained by pulling back the universal quotient. Conversely, a quotient $\mathcal E\to\mathcal L$ determines a unique $X$-morphism $X\to\mathbb P(\mathcal E)$. These constructions are inverse, with quotients identified when an isomorphism of their targets commutes with the quotient maps.
