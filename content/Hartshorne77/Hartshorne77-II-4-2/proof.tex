The equalizer of $f$ and $g$ is the closed subscheme $E=X\times_{Y\times_SY}Y$, where $X\to Y\times_SY$ is $(f,g)$ and $Y\to Y\times_SY$ is the diagonal. Its ideal sheaf restricts to zero on the given dense open set $U$. On an affine open $\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq X$, every section of this ideal vanishes on the dense set $U\cap\operatorname{Spec}A$, hence belongs to every prime of $A$. Since $A$ is reduced, that section is zero. Thus $E=X$, so $f=g$.

(a) Take $X=\operatorname{Spec}k[t,\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2,t\epsilon)$ and $Y=\mathbb A_k^1$. The maps induced by $z\mapsto0$ and $z\mapsto\epsilon$ are distinct but agree on the dense open $D(t)$.

(b) Let $Y$ be the affine line with doubled origin. Its two standard open immersions $\mathbb A_k^1\to Y$ agree off the origin but are distinct.
