Replacing $(a_0,\ldots,a_n)$ by $(\lambda a_0,\ldots,\lambda a_n)$ multiplies row $i$ of the Jacobian by $\lambda^{\deg f_i-1}$, so its rank is unchanged. Assume $a_0=1$. Euler's identity gives
\[\frac{\partial f_i}{\partial x_0}(P)=-\sum_{j=1}^n a_j\frac{\partial f_i}{\partial x_j}(P),\]
since $f_i(P)=0$. Thus the first column is a linear combination of the others, in every characteristic. Deleting it gives exactly the affine Jacobian of $f_i(1,x_1,\ldots,x_n)$. These polynomials generate the ideal of $Y\cap\{x_0\ne0\}$, so the affine Jacobian criterion gives rank $n-r$ exactly at the nonsingular points.
