If $p\nmid d$, including $p=0$, take $f=x^d+y^d+z^d$. Its partial derivatives have no common projective zero.

If $p\mid d$, take $f=x^{d-1}y+y^{d-1}z+z^d$. A common zero of $f,f_x,f_y,f_z$ satisfies $y=0$ by $f_z=y^{d-1}$, then $z=0$ by $f=0$, and finally $x=0$ by $f_y=0$. Thus there is no such projective point. In either case the gradient criterion and \exref{I.5.9} show that $Z(f)$ is an irreducible nonsingular curve of degree $d$.
