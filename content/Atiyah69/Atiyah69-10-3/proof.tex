By Krull's theorem, $x\in\bigcap_n\mathfrak{a}^nM$ exactly when $(1-a)x=0$ for some $a\in\mathfrak{a}$. Such an element maps to zero in every $M_{\mathfrak{m}}$ with $\mathfrak{m}\supseteq\mathfrak{a}$, because $1-a\notin\mathfrak{m}$.

Conversely, suppose $x$ maps to zero in every such localization. Then $\operatorname{Ann}(x)$ is not contained in any maximal ideal containing $\mathfrak{a}$. Hence $\operatorname{Ann}(x)+\mathfrak{a}=A$. Write $1=b+a$ with $b\in\operatorname{Ann}(x)$ and $a\in\mathfrak{a}$. Thus $(1-a)x=0$, and Krull's theorem gives the desired equality.

The completion is zero exactly when all $M/\mathfrak{a}^nM$ are zero: the projections from their inverse limit are surjective. Equivalently, $M=\bigcap_n\mathfrak{a}^nM$, which by the equality just proved means $M_{\mathfrak{m}}=0$ for every maximal $\mathfrak{m}\supseteq\mathfrak{a}$. Since $M$ is finitely generated, $\operatorname{Supp}M=V(\operatorname{Ann}M)$ is closed. Its intersection with $V(\mathfrak{a})$ is therefore empty exactly when it contains no maximal ideal, proving the assertion.
