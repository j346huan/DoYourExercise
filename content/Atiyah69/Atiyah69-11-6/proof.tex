A strict chain $\mathfrak p_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathfrak p_r$ in $A$ gives the strict chain
\[\mathfrak p_0[x]\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathfrak p_r[x]\subsetneq\mathfrak p_r[x]+(x)\]
in $A[x]$. The last ideal is prime because its quotient is $A/\mathfrak p_r$. Taking suprema gives $\dim A[x]\geq1+\dim A$.

For the upper bound, consider a finite strict chain in $\operatorname{Spec}A[x]$. Its distinct contractions form a strict chain in $\operatorname{Spec}A$. For a fixed contraction $\mathfrak p$, localization at $A\setminus\mathfrak p$ followed by quotienting by $\mathfrak p$ identifies the primes above $\mathfrak p$, preserving inclusions, with the primes of $\kappa(\mathfrak p)[x]$. This ring has dimension one, so at most two members of the chain have the same contraction. If $\dim A=d<\infty$, the chain consequently has at most $2(d+1)$ members, hence length at most $2d+1$. If $\dim A=\infty$, the lower bound already gives $\dim A[x]=\infty$.
