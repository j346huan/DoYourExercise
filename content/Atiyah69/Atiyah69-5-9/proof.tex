The coefficients of any element of $C[x]$ are integral over $A$, hence over $A[x]$. Since integral elements form a subring, $C[x]$ is integral over $A[x]$.

Conversely, suppose $f\in B[x]$ satisfies $F(f)=0$, where $F(T)=T^m+g_1(x)T^{m-1}+\cdots+g_m(x)$ with $g_i\in A[x]$. Choose $r$ larger than the degrees of $f$ and all $g_i$. The identity $F(x^r)-F(f)=(x^r-f)Q$ gives $F(x^r)=(x^r-f)Q$ for some $Q\in B[x]$. Here $x^r-f$ is monic of degree $r$, $F(x^r)$ is monic of degree $rm$, and therefore $Q$ is monic of degree $r(m-1)$. By \exref{5.8}, $x^r-f\in C[x]$, so $f\in C[x]$.
