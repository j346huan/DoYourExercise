If $A$ is Artinian, decompose it into finitely many Artin local rings. Each factor is still a finitely generated $k$-algebra, so it suffices to treat the local case.

Let $\mathfrak m^N=0$ and write $A=k[x_1,\ldots,x_r]$. By the weak Nullstellensatz, $A/\mathfrak m$ is finite over $k$. Thus for each $i$ there is a monic $f_i\in k[t]$ with $f_i(x_i)\in\mathfrak m$, giving $f_i(x_i)^N=0$. The monomials $\prod_i x_i^{e_i}$ with $0\leq e_i<N\deg f_i$ span $A$ over $k$, so its dimension is finite.

Conversely, ideals in a finite-dimensional $k$-algebra are vector subspaces, and therefore satisfy the descending chain condition.
