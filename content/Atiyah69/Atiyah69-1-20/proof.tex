\textbf{(i)} Any two nonempty relatively open subsets of $\overline Y$ meet $Y$. If $Y$ is irreducible, their intersections with $Y$ meet, so $\overline Y$ is irreducible.

\textbf{(ii)} The union of a chain of irreducible subspaces is irreducible: two open sets meeting the union both meet one member of the chain and therefore intersect there. Zorn's lemma gives a maximal irreducible subspace containing any given one.

\textbf{(iii)} By (i), a maximal irreducible subspace is closed. Singletons are irreducible, so these maximal subspaces cover the space. In a Hausdorff space, two distinct points have disjoint neighborhoods, so an irreducible subspace contains at most one point. The components are therefore singletons.

\textbf{(iv)} A closed subset $V(\mathfrak a)$ is homeomorphic to $\operatorname{Spec}(A/\mathfrak a)$, hence is irreducible iff $\sqrt{\mathfrak a}$ is prime by \exref{1.19}. Since inclusion reverses under $V$, the maximal irreducible closed subsets are precisely $V(\mathfrak p)$ for minimal primes $\mathfrak p$.
