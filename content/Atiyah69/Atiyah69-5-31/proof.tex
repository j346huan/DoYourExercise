We have $v(1)=0$ and $v(x^{-1})=-v(x)$. Also $2v(-1)=v(1)=0$; an ordered abelian group is torsion-free, so $v(-1)=0$. Thus $A$ contains $0,1$ and is closed under additive inverses, multiplication, and addition by the valuation inequality. Totality gives $v(x)\geq0$ or $v(x^{-1})\geq0$ for every $x\neq0$, so $A$ is a valuation ring.

An element is a unit of $A$ exactly when its value is zero. Hence $v$ induces $K^*/A^*\cong v(K^*)$, preserving order because $x/y\in A$ exactly when $v(x)\geq v(y)$.
