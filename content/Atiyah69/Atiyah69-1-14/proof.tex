The zero ideal belongs to this set. The union of a chain is again an ideal consisting of zero divisors, so Zorn's lemma gives maximal members, also among those containing any fixed such ideal.

Let $\mathfrak p$ be maximal and suppose $xy\in\mathfrak p$ with $x,y\notin\mathfrak p$. Then $(x)+\mathfrak p$ and $(y)+\mathfrak p$ contain non-zero-divisors $f,g$. Their product is a non-zero-divisor, but $fg\in(xy)+(x)\mathfrak p+(y)\mathfrak p+\mathfrak p^2\subseteq\mathfrak p$, a contradiction. Also $1\notin\mathfrak p$, so $\mathfrak p$ is prime. If $x$ is a zero divisor, every multiple of $x$ is a zero divisor, so $(x)$ is contained in one of these prime ideals.
