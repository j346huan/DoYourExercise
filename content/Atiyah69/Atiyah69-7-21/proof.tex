If $E$ is constructible, so is its intersection with $X_0$, and \exref{7.20}(ii) gives the condition.

Conversely, suppose the condition holds but $E$ is not constructible. Choose a minimal closed subset $X_0$ such that $E\cap X_0$ is not constructible. If $X_0$ were the union of two proper closed subsets, minimality would make both intersections constructible, a contradiction. Thus $X_0$ is irreducible.

If $E\cap X_0$ is not dense, its closure $C$ in $X_0$ is proper, and $E\cap X_0=E\cap C$ is constructible by minimality. Otherwise choose a nonempty open $U\subseteq X_0$ contained in $E$. The closed set $X_0\setminus U$ is proper, so $E\cap(X_0\setminus U)$ is constructible. Since $U$ is locally closed in $X$, their union $E\cap X_0$ is constructible, again a contradiction.
