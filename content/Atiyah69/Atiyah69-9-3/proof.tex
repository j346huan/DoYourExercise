In a valuation ring the principal ideals are linearly ordered: for nonzero $a,b$, either $a/b$ or $b/a$ belongs to the ring. Hence every finitely generated ideal is generated by one of its generators. If the ring is Noetherian, every ideal is therefore principal. A local principal ideal domain which is not a field has dimension one, so Proposition 9.2 makes it a discrete valuation ring.

Conversely, in a discrete valuation ring every nonzero ideal contains an element of least valuation. Every other element of the ideal is divisible by it, so the ideal is principal. Thus the ring is Noetherian.
