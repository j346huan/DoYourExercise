A convergent power series is invertible in $B$ exactly when its constant term is nonzero: its reciprocal is holomorphic near the origin. Thus $B$ is local. Its maximal ideal is $\mathfrak{m}=(z_1,\ldots,z_n)$, since a convergent series with zero constant term can be written as $\sum_i z_i g_i$ with convergent $g_i$ by grouping its monomials.

More generally, grouping monomials of total degree at least $q$ by factors of degree $q$ shows that $\mathfrak{m}^q$ consists exactly of the convergent series of order at least $q$. Truncation therefore gives $B/\mathfrak{m}^q\cong\mathbb{C}[z_1,\ldots,z_n]/(z_1,\ldots,z_n)^q$. Taking inverse limits gives $\widehat B\cong C$.

The same finite Taylor quotients identify the maximal-ideal completion of $A$ with $C$. Since $A$ is Noetherian, $C$ is flat over $A$. Assuming $B$ is Noetherian, \exref{10.7} makes $C$ faithfully flat over the local ring $B$. Faithfully flat descent of flatness, \exref{3.17}, now shows that $B$ is flat over $A$.
