Suffices to show the lattice from a boolean ring correspond to the lattice of clopen sets in its spectrum, i.e. those of form $D(f)$. This is because $D(f)\cup D(g)=D(g+f+fg)$ and $D(f)\cap D(g)=D(fg)$ as shown before, and $D(f)\subseteq D(g)$ implies a prime ideal either contains $1-g$, or it does not contain $1-g$ which implies it is not in $D(g)$, so not in $D(f)$, so it contains $f$. Therefore $f(1-g)$ is in all the prime ideals. Since there is no nontrivial nilpotent elements, $f(1-g)=0$ and $f=fg$.
