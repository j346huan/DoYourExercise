For $x\in X$, evaluation $\operatorname{ev}_x:P(X)\to k$ is well-defined because every element of $I(X)$ vanishes at $x$. Constants make it surjective, so its kernel $\mathfrak m_x$ is maximal.

If $x\neq y$, choose $i$ with $x_i\neq y_i$. The class of $t_i-x_i$ belongs to $\mathfrak m_x$ but not to $\mathfrak m_y$. Hence $x\mapsto\mathfrak m_x$ is injective.

For a maximal ideal $\mathfrak m\subseteq P(X)$, its inverse image in $k[t_1,\ldots,t_n]$ is maximal and contains $I(X)$. By \exref{5.17}, it equals $(t_1-a_1,\ldots,t_n-a_n)$ for some $a\in k^n$. Containment of $I(X)$ gives $a\in V(I(X))=X$, and then $\mathfrak m=\mathfrak m_a$. Thus the evaluation map is surjective.
