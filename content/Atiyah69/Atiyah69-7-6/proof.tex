Suppose $K$ has characteristic zero. Then $\mathbb{Z}\subseteq\mathbb{Q}\subseteq K$, and $K$ is a finitely generated $\mathbb{Q}$-algebra. By Proposition 7.9, it is finite over $\mathbb{Q}$. The Artin–Tate lemma would make $\mathbb{Q}$ finitely generated over $\mathbb{Z}$. This is impossible: the denominators of finitely many rational generators involve only finitely many primes, whereas $\mathbb{Q}$ contains their reciprocals for every prime.

Thus $K$ has characteristic $p>0$ and is a finitely generated $\mathbb{F}_p$-algebra. Proposition 7.9 makes it a finite-dimensional $\mathbb{F}_p$-vector space, so it is finite.
