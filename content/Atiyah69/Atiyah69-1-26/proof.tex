Evaluation at $x$ is a surjection onto $\mathbb R$, so $\mathfrak m_x$ is maximal. If a maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$ had no common zero, compactness would give $f_1,\ldots,f_n\in\mathfrak m$ with no common zero. Then $\sum_i f_i^2$ would be everywhere positive and hence a unit, a contradiction. Thus $\mathfrak m\subseteq\mathfrak m_x$ for some $x$, and maximality gives equality. This proves surjectivity.

Urysohn's lemma separates distinct points by continuous functions, so their evaluation ideals are distinct. Thus the map is bijective.

The sets $\{x:f(x)\ne0\}$ form a basis: for $x\in U$ open, Urysohn's lemma gives $f(x)=1$ and $f=0$ on $X\setminus U$. Under the bijection these sets correspond exactly to $D(f)\cap\operatorname{Max}C(X)$, which form a basis of the maximal spectrum. Hence the map is a homeomorphism.
