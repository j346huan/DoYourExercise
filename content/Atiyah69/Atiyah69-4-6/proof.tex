By \exref{1.26}, the maximal ideals are $\mathfrak{m}_x=\{f:f(x)=0\}$ for $x\in X$. A prime cannot lie in two distinct maximal ideals $\mathfrak{m}_x,\mathfrak{m}_y$: normality gives continuous functions $f,g$ with disjoint supports and $f(x)=g(y)=1$, so $fg=0$ although neither factor belongs to that prime. Hence every prime, and therefore every primary ideal, is contained in a unique maximal ideal.

If $(0)=\bigcap_{i=1}^n\mathfrak{q}_i$ were a primary decomposition, every maximal ideal would contain one of the $\mathfrak{q}_i$, since it contains their product. There would then be at most $n$ maximal ideals, contradicting the infinitude of $X$. Thus $(0)$ is not decomposable.
