For any three lattice elements, the eight meets obtained by choosing each element or its complement are pairwise disjoint and have join $1$. Each of the three elements is the join of the corresponding meets. Distributivity therefore reduces all ring identities involving them to identities of subsets of these eight pieces: multiplication is intersection and addition is symmetric difference. This proves associativity, commutativity and distributivity. The identities are $0,1$, each element is its own additive inverse, and $a^2=a$.

Conversely, $a=ab$ defines a partial order: reflexivity follows from $a^2=a$, antisymmetry from commutativity, and transitivity from $a=ab$, $b=bc\Rightarrow a=ac$. The element $ab$ is the greatest lower bound. Since $a(a+b+ab)=a$ and $b(a+b+ab)=b$, the element $a+b+ab$ is an upper bound; any common upper bound $c$ satisfies $c(a+b+ab)=a+b+ab$, so it is the least one. Both distributive laws follow by expansion using $2a=0$ and $a^2=a$.

The complement is $1-a$. If $c$ is another complement, then $ac=0$ and $a+c+ac=1$, so $c=1-a$. Thus the lattice is Boolean. The recovered multiplication is unchanged, and the recovered addition is $a(1-b)+(1-a)b=a+b$. Starting with a lattice, its meet and order are recovered, hence so are its join and complements.
