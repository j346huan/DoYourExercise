Choose generators $m_1,\ldots,m_n$ of $M$. The map $A\to M^n$, $a\mapsto(am_1,\ldots,am_n)$, has kernel $\mathfrak a$, so $A/\mathfrak a$ embeds in the Noetherian module $M^n$. Its $A$-submodules are exactly its $A/\mathfrak a$-submodules, so $A/\mathfrak a$ is Noetherian.

The Artinian assertion is false. Fix a prime $p$ and take the $\mathbb Z$-module $\mathbb Z[1/p]/\mathbb Z$. Its subgroup of elements killed by $p^n$ is cyclic of order $p^n$. Any subgroup with elements of unbounded order contains all these cyclic subgroups and is therefore the whole module. Hence every proper subgroup is finite, and every descending chain stabilizes. The module is faithful, since no nonzero integer annihilates elements of arbitrarily large $p$-power order. However, $\mathbb Z$ is not Artinian, as witnessed by $\mathbb Z\supsetneq2\mathbb Z\supsetneq4\mathbb Z\supsetneq\cdots$.
