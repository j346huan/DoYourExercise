Write $\mu_i,\nu_i,\rho_i$ for the structure maps and $\psi,\varphi$ for the induced maps. Since $\varphi_i\psi_i=0$, we have $\varphi\psi\mu_i=\rho_i\varphi_i\psi_i=0$ for every $i$, hence $\operatorname{im}\psi\subseteq\ker\varphi$.

Suppose $\varphi(\nu_i(y))=\rho_i(\varphi_i(y))=0$. By \exref{2.15}, some $j\geq i$ satisfies $\rho_{ij}(\varphi_i(y))=\varphi_j(\nu_{ij}(y))=0$. Exactness at $N_j$ gives $x\in M_j$ with $\psi_j(x)=\nu_{ij}(y)$. Thus $\nu_i(y)=\nu_j(\psi_j(x))=\psi(\mu_j(x))$. This proves the reverse inclusion.
