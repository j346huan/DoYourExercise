Translate $P$ to the origin and put $\mathfrak n=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ in $k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$. Its localization is regular of dimension $n$. Since $f$ is a nonzero nonunit in this domain, Corollary 11.18 gives $\dim A_{\mathfrak m}=n-1$.

Localization does not change the cotangent space, and
\[\mathfrak mA_{\mathfrak m}/(\mathfrak mA_{\mathfrak m})^2\cong\mathfrak n/(\mathfrak n^2+(f)).\]
The space $\mathfrak n/\mathfrak n^2$ has basis the classes of $x_1,\ldots,x_n$. The image of $(f)$ in it is spanned by the linear part $\sum_i(\partial f/\partial x_i)(P)x_i$. Thus the displayed space has dimension $n-1$ if some partial derivative is nonzero at $P$, and dimension $n$ otherwise. By Theorem 11.22, $A_{\mathfrak m}$ is regular exactly in the first case.
